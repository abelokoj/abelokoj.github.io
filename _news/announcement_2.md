In 2017 I was awarded a gold medal at the national mathematics competition for university
undergraduates, held at the National Mathematical Centre in Abuja. Competing as a
400-level undergraduate representing the Federal University of Petroleum Resources,
Effurun (FUPRE), I placed third overall among 126 contestants in the gold medal category.
On the strength of that result I was named to the four-member national team selected to
represent Nigeria at the international undergraduate mathematics competition held later
that year in Poland. FUPRE finished eleventh in the institutional standings, an
improvement on its position in the previous year.

The result followed a silver medal at the same competition in 2016, which I had contested
a year early, at the 300 level, against candidates in their final year.

### About the competition

The competition is an annual national contest in mathematics for university
undergraduates in Nigeria, convened at the National Mathematical Centre (NMC) in Abuja.
The Centre is the federal institution charged with advancing mathematical research and
training in Nigeria, and the competition serves as its principal instrument for
identifying and benchmarking undergraduate mathematical talent across the university
system.

Candidates are nominated by their home institutions and sit a common written examination.
Individual performance is recognised through graded medal categories — gold, silver, and
bronze — with placements determined by rank within each category; the 2017 edition drew
126 contestants. Results are aggregated to produce an institutional ranking alongside the
individual awards, so that departments as well as students are measured against a
national standard.

The competition also functions as a national selection trial. The strongest performers
are invited to form the small team that represents Nigeria at international undergraduate
mathematics competitions, which in 2017 was a four-member delegation.
