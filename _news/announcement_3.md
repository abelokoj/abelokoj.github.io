I was selected as an Opportunity Funds scholar by EducationUSA Nigeria, in the cohort
advised out of the Abuja centre. The programme underwrites the up-front costs of applying
to graduate study in the United States for candidates who are academically competitive but
unable to meet those costs privately, and it was on that support that I assembled and
submitted my doctoral applications.

The applications returned four fully funded doctoral offers, of which I accepted Florida
State University's, entering the PhD programme in Applied and Computational Mathematics.
At the point of selection I had graduated from the Federal University of Petroleum
Resources, Effurun (FUPRE), with a cumulative grade point average of 4.95 on a 5.00 scale
— the highest recorded at the university since its establishment, and first in class —
alongside a gold medal from the national undergraduate mathematics competition and two
technical publications. EducationUSA Nigeria profiled the outcome publicly in April 2021.

### About the programme

Opportunity Funds is a programme of the United States Department of State, delivered
through the EducationUSA network of advising centres that the Department maintains in
countries around the world. It addresses a narrow but decisive obstacle in international
graduate admissions: the expenses that fall due *before* any funding decision is made.
Standardised testing, application fees, credential evaluation, visa processing, and
airfare must all be paid in advance, and for otherwise strong candidates these costs are
frequently prohibitive — with the result that the applicant pool is filtered by ability to
pay well before it is filtered by academic merit.

The programme covers those preparatory costs for a small number of candidates judged
likely to win full financial support from United States institutions on the strength of
their record. Selection is therefore concentrated on applicants whose academic standing is
already competitive and for whom the financial barrier is the binding constraint. Because
the intended outcome is admission with full funding, the programme is best understood as
removing a structural barrier to access rather than as a scholarship toward tuition.

Candidates are identified and advised through the EducationUSA centres in their own
country, which also provide guidance on institution selection, application strategy, and
the admissions process itself.
